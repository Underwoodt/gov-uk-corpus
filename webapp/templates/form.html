One term per line, or separated by commas. A term is a single word, or a phrase whose words sit next to each other.

How your terms are matched

We look through the title and text of every page for the terms you list. A page with none of them is skipped.

A single word (slurry) matches anywhere. A multi-word term is matched as a phrase — the words must appear together, in order (catch certificate matches “a catch certificate”, but not a page that merely mentions “catch” and “certificate” far apart). A small linking word between them is allowed (food standards still matches “food and standards”). Word endings are matched, so lagoon also finds lagoons. Capital letters do not matter.

  • Avoid broad single words such as farm, water, waste — they match thousands of pages.
  • Prefer a specific word or phrase: slurry, lagoon, catch certificate.

After saving, also search GOV.UK's own Search for these keywords (scoped to the organisations and document types above), tag pages by provenance, and fetch any GOV.UK-only pages into the corpus so the AI Pipeline can evaluate them. Runs in the background — it can take a few minutes — and its results appear on the Hybrid Search tab.

Describe your topic in your own words. Be generous: this is about keeping anything relevant.

How to write this well

Say what the thing is. Do not insist the page is mainly about your topic — a single mention can still be the page you need. Do not list what you are not interested in; that belongs in "What to exclude".

Slurry is liquid or semi-liquid livestock manure: cattle and pig slurry, dirty water, digestate from farm anaerobic digestion, and silage effluent. Include a page if it mentions slurry in this farming sense anywhere, even once.

List the things that look relevant but are not. Be as specific as you can.

How to write this well

Words with another meaning (coal slurry), the wrong subject area (sewage sludge when you mean animal slurry), or pages that mention your topic once in passing. Being concrete works better.

Exclude pages where slurry means something other than livestock manure: coal, mining, concrete slurry. Exclude sewage sludge and biosolids.
